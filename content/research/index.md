My research uses gravitational-wave observations to investigate compact objects, dark matter and fundamental physics, and develops data-analysis methods to detect weak, persistent and long-duration signals. Learn more about our team on the [Gravatars website](https://the-gravatars.github.io/).

## Neutron stars

Continuous gravitational waves provide a way to probe non-axisymmetric neutron stars, their internal structure and rotational dynamics. My research investigates persistent emission from isolated and accreting neutron stars, long-duration signals from newborn neutron stars, and the potential of future detectors to measure compact-object properties. I also investigate early warning and sky localization of binary neutron stars using their long inspiral signals.

## Primordial black holes and subsolar-mass binaries

Primordial black holes could constitute a fraction of dark matter. Low-mass compact binaries can emit signals that persist in the detector band for hours or longer, motivating searches beyond conventional compact-binary pipelines. My work develops searches for planetary-mass and subsolar-mass binaries, constrains primordial black hole populations, and explores mini-extreme-mass-ratio inspirals and dark-matter environments.

## Direct detection of ultralight dark matter

Gravitational-wave interferometers can act as particle detectors: ultralight fields may produce oscillatory forces or changes in material properties that generate measurable instrumental responses. My research develops methods to distinguish and constrain different dark matter interactions using ground-based and space-based instruments.

## Black hole superradiance and boson clouds

Ultralight bosons can form clouds around rotating black holes through superradiance. Their gravitational-wave emission offers a way to probe particle masses and black hole populations. My work includes search methodologies and analyses targeting scalar and vector clouds around isolated and merger-remnant black holes. Related research directions include gravitational atoms and molecules.

## Gravitational-wave backgrounds and cosmology

Stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds can encode information about unresolved astrophysical populations and early-Universe physics. My research interests include using background searches to constrain neutron star populations and investigating prospects for probing fundamental physics and cosmological phase transitions.

## Gravitational-wave data analysis

Long-duration signals pose computational and statistical challenges. I develop semicoherent and pattern-recognition methods, including generalized frequency-Hough transforms, robust candidate selection and machine learning, to search large parameter spaces and distinguish weak signals from instrumental disturbances.
